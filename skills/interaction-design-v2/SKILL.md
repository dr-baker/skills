# Interaction design V2

- Build a custom interface whenever it can meaningfully make the task easier to understand or perform.
- Cut menu text aggressively. Use recognizable icons, visual previews, direct manipulation, and clear grouping. Keep short labels where meaning would otherwise be ambiguous; give icons accessible names.
- Make layout do the explaining. Use hierarchy, spacing, and proximity to show what belongs together and what happens next.
- Stage attention like a Disney park. Draw the eye to the most important thing, then guide it toward the next useful discovery.
- Reveal complexity progressively. Start with a clear entry point; let deeper controls appear naturally as intent develops. Keep expert capabilities reachable.
- Replace piles of knobs with meaningful choices, coordinated controls, and useful defaults.
- Teach through doing. Use examples, immediate feedback, and contextual help when needed. Make guidance easy to revisit.
- Reuse components and interaction patterns so familiar objects behave consistently everywhere.
- Keep controls beside what they affect. Show results promptly; preserve focus, selection, and unfinished work.
- Review the finished product through the audience's eyes. What do they know, misunderstand, notice first, and need next? Walk through the real task and fix the friction.
